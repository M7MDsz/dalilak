Masari · visual direction "lawha" · the game-maker's board

A career played on a board. Every stage is a tile, the road is printed across the tiles, and one ember piece moves along it: you. Ink outlines, flat colour, hard offset shadows with zero blur; colour is rationed so that each hue means one thing.

The mark is the letter Meem drawn the way roads are drawn on a game board. Its eye is a tile that holds your piece; its tail is a road that climbs one step to the left, the way Arabic reads ("daraja" means both a stair step and a level). The ember dot is you: it lives in the logo, appears once in every icon, and moves across the board as you progress. One-colour and small-size cuts keep the same three parts.

What moves, and how. The showcase is one turn of the game, about 12 s, then 2 s of rest, then again. (1) An ember line reads down the CV sheet; each highlighted line throws a skill chip that hops out and lands (three chips, 300 ms apart). (2) The stamp comes down on the finished tile, and the piece hops along the stair-shaped road onto the next-step card: a 400 ms arc, a hard stop, the card dips 4 px under it. (3) The +200 XP coin drops onto the bar; the bar fills in four chunky steps and the match ring inks its next segment. (4) Sara's leaderboard row lifts, slides up one place over the row above and lands; the badge is stamped. Dashed outlines in this still are the positions each piece has just left. Nothing drifts, floats or glows: between beats every piece is still. Reduced motion shows the finished turn with no loop.
