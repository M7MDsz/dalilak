Order product-discovery → product-roadmap → product-specs → product-analytics is supported, with one caveat. Discovery first: Pragmatic says of Market Problems «Finding them is the foundation for everything else in the Framework.», and on roadmap.sh User Research comes before everything about strategy and requirements. Roadmap before specs: roadmap.sh puts Vision & Mission, Strategic Thinking and Defining Goals above Product Requirements; the Scrum Guide lists «Developing and explicitly communicating the Product Goal;» before «Creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog items;»; Pragmatic's course order is «Focus: Strategic Product Planning» before «Build: Product Building Course» («hand off market information ... so development can build the right products»). Analytics last: roadmap.sh puts Key Product Metrics and Data-Driven Decision Making after requirements, roadmap, release and go-to-market. Caveat: in the Pragmatic Framework the Business category (with Measurement) sits before Planning (with Requirements), and on roadmap.sh Product Requirements and Product Roadmap are side by side, so specs and roadmap are close to parallel.

Order supply-chain → procurement is NOT supported by the guidelines that opened. CIPS teaches both together from the start («L2M1 Introducing Procurement and Supply»), puts procurement operations second («L2M2 Procurement and Supply Operations») and puts the supply-chain-operations module last («L2M5 Inventory, Logistics and Expediting»). ASCM's CPIM/CSCP outlines, which would describe the supply-chain side, did not open. For procurement jobs (most of the openings found), procurement could come first, or the two can be treated as parallel; Saudi government procurement (Etimad, the procurement law) belongs inside procurement.
